## 1. Welcome and company context

Welcome to the fictional internal team behind Devscale Indonesia. Publicly, Devscale focuses on helping students build strong software-engineering fundamentals, learn through practical projects, receive mentor feedback, and use modern AI tools responsibly.

Our mock internal mission is to help people become practical software engineers for the AI era by combining clear instruction, realistic building experience, thoughtful feedback, and sound technical judgment.

| Value | What it looks like at work |
| --- | --- |
| Clarity | Write decisions down, explain the reason, define the next action, and reduce avoidable confusion. |
| Momentum | Prefer small, reviewable progress and short feedback loops over large invisible efforts. |
| Craft | Treat code, teaching, communication, and product judgment as connected professional skills. |
| Care | Respect learners and colleagues, protect their data, and give feedback without humiliation. |

The directly responsible individual proposes a decision in writing. Affected teammates comment within the stated review window. The owner records the final choice, reason, date, and follow-up action. Reversible decisions should move quickly; security, legal, and learner-safety decisions require explicit review.

## 2. Employment basics

Employees are responsible for keeping their legal name, address, emergency contact, bank account, and tax information current. Mock changes should be submitted to People Operations within five working days.

The sample introductory period is three months. Managers hold check-ins at the end of weeks 2, 6, and 10. The purpose is alignment and support, not surprise evaluation.

| Classification | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Full-time | A regular role scheduled for 40 hours per week. |
| Part-time | A regular role with a documented schedule below 40 hours per week. |
| Fixed-term | A role with a written end date or defined project period. |
| Contractor | An independent service provider governed by a services agreement, not this handbook. |

Employees must disclose outside work, investments, close personal relationships, or gifts that could influence a Devscale decision. Teaching, open-source work, and public speaking are encouraged when they do not use confidential information or interfere with agreed responsibilities.

Access to personnel records is limited to the employee, People Operations, and managers with a legitimate need. Employee information must not be copied into public chat channels or unapproved AI services.

## 3. Remote work and working hours

This fictional workplace is remote-first within Indonesia. Employees may work from a safe location with reliable internet and reasonable privacy. Working from another country for up to 20 calendar days requires written manager and People Operations approval before travel.

- Normal workload: 40 hours per week, Monday through Friday.
- Core collaboration hours: 10:00–15:00 WIB, Monday through Thursday.
- Friday focus period: no recurring internal meetings after 13:00 WIB.
- Employees arrange the remaining hours with their manager and document any recurring alternative schedule.

Employees should update their calendar and team status when unavailable for more than 60 minutes during core hours. Unexpected absence should be reported to the manager by 09:30 WIB whenever reasonably possible.

Every meeting needs an owner, purpose, and expected decision or outcome. Default meeting lengths are 25 or 50 minutes. Notes and actions are posted within one working day. Attendance is optional when an employee can contribute asynchronously.

Planned work outside an employee's normal schedule requires manager approval. Production incidents may require urgent response; the incident lead records participants and arranges appropriate recovery time under the applicable agreement.

## 4. Pay, benefits, and expenses

Monthly payroll is processed on the 25th calendar day. If the 25th falls on a weekend or public holiday, payment is processed on the preceding banking day. Payroll questions should be raised within five working days of receiving the payslip.

| Benefit | Mock allowance | Key rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Work equipment | Up to IDR 15,000,000 every 36 months | Manager approval before purchase; company-owned items are returned at offboarding. |
| Learning budget | Up to IDR 6,000,000 per calendar year | Must support the employee's role or agreed growth plan. |
| Internet support | IDR 500,000 per month | Paid with payroll; no receipt required in this mock policy. |
| Co-working | Up to IDR 750,000 per month | Receipt required; cannot be exchanged for cash. |

Approved business expenses must be submitted within 10 calendar days of purchase. A submission includes the receipt, business purpose, project or cost center, and approving manager. Finance targets reimbursement within seven working days after approval.

Travel requires written approval before booking. Employees should choose reasonable economy transport and practical accommodation. Personal upgrades are paid by the employee. Travel advances must be reconciled within five working days after return.

## 5. Time off and wellbeing

| Leave type | Mock entitlement | Notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual leave | 15 working days per calendar year | Request 5 working days ahead when taking 3 or more consecutive days. |
| Personal wellbeing leave | 3 working days per year | Same-day notice is allowed. |
| Sick leave | As documented in the employee's agreement | Notify manager by 09:30 WIB; supporting documentation after the third consecutive day. |
| Compassionate leave | 5 working days per event | Notify manager as soon as practical. |
| Volunteer day | 1 working day per year | Request 10 working days ahead. |

Managers should approve leave unless there is a specific coverage risk. Teams maintain a shared coverage plan. No employee should be expected to monitor routine messages while on approved leave.

Up to five unused annual-leave days may carry into the next calendar year. Carried days should be used by 31 March or they expire, unless People Operations grants a written exception.

Employees may mute non-urgent notifications outside their working schedule. Managers must not treat immediate after-hours replies as a performance signal. Anyone experiencing sustained overload should raise it during the weekly check-in or directly with People Operations.

## 6. Performance and growth

- Weekly manager check-in: priorities, blockers, workload, and feedback.
- Quarterly growth conversation: progress against role expectations and learning goals.
- Semiannual review: written self-reflection, manager assessment, and next-period goals.

Performance is evaluated across outcomes, reliability, collaboration, communication, craft, and learning. Visible activity, message volume, and hours online are not standalone measures of impact.

Useful feedback is specific, timely, and connected to an observable behavior or outcome. The receiver should understand what happened, why it matters, and what could be tried next. Sensitive feedback is delivered privately.

For the learning budget, the employee submits the learning goal, resource, cost, and expected application. The manager responds within five working days. After completion, the employee shares a short note, demo, or team lesson.

Promotion is based on sustained work at the next level, not tenure alone. A promotion proposal includes evidence from at least two projects and feedback from relevant collaborators. Compensation changes are confirmed in writing by People Operations.

## 7. Engineering and product practices

Work is done when the agreed behavior is implemented, relevant checks pass, documentation is updated, operational impact is considered, and the change is reviewable by another team member.

- Normal changes require at least one approving reviewer.
- Authentication, billing, permissions, migrations, or destructive data operations require two approving reviewers.
- Authors should keep changes focused and explain the problem, approach, verification, and risk.
- Reviewers comment on correctness and user impact before personal style preferences.

Every bug fix should include a regression check when practical. High-risk user flows need automated coverage or a documented manual verification plan. Tests must not depend on production customer data.

Deployments should be observable and reversible. The owner monitors the release, records material issues, and prepares a rollback path for high-risk changes. Feature flags are preferred when a staged release reduces risk.

Architecture decisions, runbooks, setup instructions, and recurring operational knowledge belong in the shared documentation system, not only in private messages. Owners review critical runbooks every six months.

## 8. Responsible AI use

AI tools can accelerate exploration, drafting, coding, and review, but the employee remains responsible for the final work. AI output is a proposal to verify, not evidence that a task is correct.

| Class | Examples | AI rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green | Public documentation, synthetic examples, published marketing content | May be used with approved AI tools. |
| Yellow | Internal plans, unpublished lessons, private source code without secrets | Use only approved enterprise tools with retention controls. |
| Red | Passwords, API keys, payment data, government identifiers, private learner records | Never submit to a generative AI tool. |

- Run and review AI-generated code before merging it.
- Verify factual claims against primary sources.
- Check licenses and attribution before using generated or retrieved material.
- Use human review for decisions affecting access, employment, assessment, or learner safety.

Material AI assistance should be disclosed in the pull request, document note, or project record when it affects implementation or content. Disclosure can be short: tool used, purpose, and verification performed.

Never paste secrets into prompts. If a secret is exposed, rotate it immediately and report a security incident; deleting the conversation is not sufficient.

## 9. Security and data handling

- Use the company password manager and a unique password for every service.
- Enable multi-factor authentication wherever available.
- Never share individual accounts or authentication codes.
- Lock devices when unattended and install required security updates within seven days.

Access follows least privilege. Managers review privileged access every quarter. Temporary access must have an owner and expiration date. Access that is no longer needed should be removed within one working day.

| Data type | Example handling |
| --- | --- |
| Public | May be shared externally after normal content review. |
| Internal | Share only with team members and approved collaborators. |
| Confidential | Encrypt in transit and at rest; limit access to named roles. |
| Restricted | Do not copy to personal devices, public links, or unapproved services. |

Report suspected account compromise, leaked secrets, unauthorized access, lost devices, or accidental data sharing immediately in the fictional **#security-incident** channel and by email to **security@example.devscale.id**. Do not wait to confirm the full impact before reporting.

Business records belong in approved systems with managed backups. Local files and chat messages are not a backup strategy.

## 10. Conduct, reporting, and safety

Harassment, discrimination, bullying, retaliation, threats, and deliberate humiliation are not acceptable in meetings, messages, code review, teaching, events, or social spaces connected to work.

When working with learners, use clear expectations and evaluate work consistently. Give corrective feedback privately unless a shared lesson can be discussed without identifying or embarrassing anyone. Keep professional boundaries, use approved communication channels, and escalate safety, safeguarding, or serious wellbeing concerns to the program lead.

Employees may report concerns to their manager, People Operations, or the fictional confidential address **report@example.devscale.id**. Reports should include what happened, when, who was involved, available evidence, and any immediate safety concern. Anonymous reports may be reviewed when sufficient details are provided.

Retaliation against someone who raises a good-faith concern or participates in a review is prohibited. A knowingly false allegation may itself be addressed as misconduct, but an unproven good-faith report is not a false allegation.

Personal safety comes first. Contact local emergency services for immediate danger, then notify the manager when safe. For a service incident, the incident commander coordinates response, communication, and the retrospective.

## 11. Onboarding and offboarding

Before day one, confirm signed documents, payroll information, equipment, accounts, and the first-week calendar. Assign a manager and onboarding buddy. Grant only the minimum access required for the first week.

| Period | Expected focus |
| --- | --- |
| Days 1–30 | Learn the product, users, tools, standards, and team operating rhythm. Complete one small supported contribution. |
| Days 31–60 | Own a bounded project, participate in reviews, and document one improvement opportunity. |
| Days 61–90 | Deliver independently at role scope and agree on the next two quarterly growth goals. |

The manager creates an offboarding transition plan covering work status, owners, documentation, and stakeholder communication. Company property must be returned by the final working day unless another written arrangement is approved.

People Operations confirms the final date with IT. Standard access is removed by the end of the final working day. High-risk or privileged access may be removed earlier based on the transition plan. Shared credentials must be rotated.

Departing employees update runbooks, identify open decisions, transfer owned accounts, and record the next action for active work. Confidential information remains protected after employment ends.
